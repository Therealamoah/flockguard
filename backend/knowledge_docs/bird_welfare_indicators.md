# Auditing Tools and Animal Welfare Indicators for Broiler Chickens (excerpt)

Authors: Samantha Vitek and Leonie Jacobs, School of Animal Sciences, Virginia Tech. Virginia Cooperative Extension, Publication APSC-205P (expert reviewed).

Note: the original publication is primarily a comparison of formal third-party certification audit standards (including slaughter-plant metrics like stunning/scalding/shackling effectiveness). Those slaughter-specific tables are not relevant to day-to-day farm monitoring and are omitted here - this excerpt keeps only the on-farm, farmer-observable indicators.

## Inputs vs. Animal-Based Indicators

Animal welfare audits use two categories of measures:

- **Inputs (resource-based measures)**: aspects of the environment birds are raised in - resources and management they're reared with (e.g. stocking density, litter quality, handling techniques, veterinary care practices). These can impact welfare but don't directly show the welfare state of an individual animal.
- **Animal-based indicators**: focus on the health, behavior, and affective experiences of the animal itself - a more direct reflection of actual welfare state, showing how animals are coping with the conditions provided.

To identify a welfare problem and its risk factors, **both** need to be considered together - inputs alone can miss what's actually happening to the birds, and animal-based indicators alone don't explain why a problem is occurring.

## Footpad Dermatitis (on-farm indicator)

A commonly audited animal-based indicator, typically scored on a 3-point scale (0 = no lesion, higher scores = more severe lesions), sampled at the flock or a subsample of paws. Different certification programs use different sample sizes and pass/fail thresholds, but footpad dermatitis is consistently treated as a meaningful welfare/litter-quality signal worth checking during a flock inspection - elevated levels often correlate with wet litter and housing/management issues (see also water and litter management guidance).

## Gait Score / Lameness (on-farm indicator)

Assessed using a walking-ability scoring system (commonly a 3-point scale, e.g. Webster et al. 2008), typically sampled across a subset of birds per flock (25 birds up to the whole flock, depending on the program). Reduced mobility/lameness is a direct animal-based welfare indicator and a practical thing to observe during a flock walk-through: birds reluctant or unable to walk normally, sitting excessively, or showing abnormal posture are the signal being scored.

## Practical takeaway for daily/inspection observation

When walking a house, footpad condition and gait/mobility are two concrete, observable animal-based welfare indicators (distinct from purely environmental "inputs" like stocking density) that a farmer can note during an inspection - both are used across all major U.S. broiler welfare certification programs referenced in the source publication (American Humane Certified, Certified Humane, Farm Animal Care Training and Auditing, Global Animal Partnership, National Chicken Council).
