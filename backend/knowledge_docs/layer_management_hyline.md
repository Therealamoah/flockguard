# Hy-Line W-36 Commercial Layers Management Guide (excerpt)

Publisher: Hy-Line International (W-36 Commercial Layers Management Guide, North America Edition, January 2020).

Note: the full guide (40 pages) covers house design, lighting programs, nutrition by production phase, and egg production targets in detail. This excerpt keeps the transfer-to-laying-house and mortality-monitoring guidance most relevant to day-to-day flock health monitoring.

## Transfer to the Laying House (approx. 15-16 weeks of age)

- Delayed transfer causes overcrowding in rearing cages and loss of flock uniformity and body condition.
- Rearing and production cages should use similar feed and water systems to minimize stress at transfer.
- Supportive care (water-soluble vitamins, probiotics, vitamin C, increased dietary density) 3 days before and 3 days after transfer helps reduce stress.
- Weigh birds prior to transfer and monitor weight loss during transfer - birds can lose up to ~0.25 lb (113 g) during transfer, but should recover this within one week. Unrecovered weight loss beyond about a week is a signal worth investigating.
- Monitor flock water consumption frequently after transfer - pre-transfer consumption levels should be reached again within 6 hours of arrival at the laying house. Slower recovery than that is a signal of a transfer/environment problem.
- Brighten lights for 3 days post-transfer to help birds settle into the new environment.
- **Inspect the flock and remove mortality daily.**
- **If mortality exceeds 0.1% per week, perform necropsies and other diagnostics to determine the cause(s).** This is Hy-Line's own stated action threshold - i.e., not every mortality uptick needs a full diagnostic workup, but crossing this rate specifically should trigger one.
- Transfer all birds from a house the same day, ideally early morning, so birds can keep to a normal daily routine.
- Monitor body weights weekly from 0-30 weeks of age and before any scheduled diet change.
- Body weight gain and uniformity can be negatively affected by inappropriate diet changes, handling, vaccination, and transfer itself.
- Flocks should be at approximately 90% uniformity at the time of transfer to the laying facility - uniformity is itself a tracked welfare/performance metric, not just an average bodyweight number.

## Practical takeaway

Two concrete, checkable thresholds from this source: (1) mortality above 0.1% per week for a layer flock is Hy-Line's own trigger point for diagnostic follow-up (necropsy), not just closer observation; (2) water consumption after a stressful event (like transfer) should return to baseline within a bounded time window (6 hours in the transfer case) - a slower recovery is itself a signal, independent of the absolute consumption number.
