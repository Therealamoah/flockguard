## Water Management

**Mineral content**: broilers tolerate high levels of some minerals (e.g. calcium, sodium) but are sensitive to others. Iron and manganese can give water a bitter taste that reduces consumption, support bacterial growth, and cause mineral buildup in water lines - filtration (40-50 micron mesh, checked/cleaned at least weekly) and chlorination are effective controls. Calcium and magnesium (measured as hardness) can form scale/deposits that compromise drinker systems, especially closed systems; water softeners can help, but sodium levels should be checked before using a salt-based softener. Nitrates as low as 10 ppm can impede broiler performance (note: this is a stricter threshold than some other sources cite for direct health effects - treat as a performance/precautionary guideline) - water should be tested for nitrates since elevated levels may indicate sewage or fertilizer contamination.

**Monitoring water consumption (Animal Welfare Tips from the source)**: birds consume most of their daily water requirement 2-3 hours after eating. A drop in consumption could indicate a restriction or supply issue. Evaluate daily total water consumption AND bird behavior together in each house. If there is any dramatic, unexpected change in daily water consumption, investigate:
- Feed (quality and amount consumed)
- Temperature
- Drinker maintenance (pressure, flow rate, leaks, air blockages, stray voltage)
- Water quality
- Bird health status

**Water sanitation (ORP/chlorination)**: Oxidation-Reduction Potential (ORP) reflects how strong an oxidizer the water's chlorine sanitizer currently is. An ORP of 650 mV or greater indicates good quality water; a lower value indicates a heavy organic load likely to overwhelm chlorine's ability to disinfect. Standard swimming-pool chlorine test kits don't distinguish free vs. bound chlorine, so a kit reading of 4-6 ppm can still mean no effective free chlorine is actually present if organic load is high. Chlorine is most effective at a water pH of 5.0-6.5 (a higher percentage of active hypochlorous ions, the strong sanitizer form); inorganic acids like sodium bisulfate can reduce pH without tainting the water. At least 85% hypochlorous acid should be present for a free chlorine residual reading to be considered a useful sanitizer level.

## Daily/Ongoing Record-Keeping

The guide's own broiler farm record template tracks, per day of age, throughout the flock cycle:

- Mortality, small culls, leg culls, cumulative mortality
- Bodyweight
- Water consumption, feed consumption
- Temperature (min/max), relative humidity (min/max)
- Hours of dark (lighting program)
- CO2, ammonia

This is a useful cross-check for what a farm's own daily records should realistically capture - mortality and culls broken out separately (rather than lumped together), plus environmental readings (temperature, humidity, CO2, ammonia) alongside consumption data, since these interact (e.g. elevated CO2 above ~3,000 ppm is associated with reduced bird activity and feed consumption in the source guide's environmental management sections).
